### 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?
* **Scientific Name:** *Ipomoea batatas* cultivar Xushu 18.
* **Accession Number:** NC_026703.1.

---

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?
* **Total Length:** 161,303 base pairs.

---

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.
* **Positions:** Yes. The genome exhibits a quadripartite structure consisting of a Large Single-Copy (LSC) region and a Small Single-Copy (SSC) region separated by a pair of symmetrical Inverted Repeat regions ($\text{IR}_A$ and $\text{IR}_B$) located on opposite sides of the circular map.

---

### 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.
1. ***rbcL*** (Ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase large chain)
2. ***psaA*** (Photosystem I P700 chlorophyll a apoprotein A1)
3. ***rpoB*** (RNA polymerase beta subunit)

---

### 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.
* ***ndhF*** (NADH dehydrogenase subunit F)
---

### 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?
* **Example:** ***ndhB*** (or ribosomal RNA gene ***rrn23***).
* **Duplication:** Yes, the gene appears twice on the map because it is encoded within both symmetrical Inverted Repeat regions ($\text{IR}_A$ and $\text{IR}_B$).

---

### 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.
* ***psbA**: Encodes the D1 reaction center protein of Photosystem II, which plays a critical role in light-driven water splitting and photosynthetic electron transport.

---

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?
* **Transcription Direction:** The arrows indicate the 5' to 3' direction of transcription and show whether genes are transcribed clockwise or counterclockwise along the circular genome map.

---

### 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.
* **Observation:** The GC content varies across regions; the inner graph displays distinct peaks with higher GC content values in the Inverted Repeat ($\text{IR}$) regions compared to the single-copy regions.

---

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?
* **Utility:** A graphical map provides an immediate, intuitive visual overview of genomic architecture, gene clustering by functional groups, transcriptional orientation, and structural boundaries (such as LSC/SSC/IR junctions) that are difficult to interpret from raw text sequences or flat annotation tables[cite: 5].
